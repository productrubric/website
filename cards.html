Copy the prompt below, paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, or another AI tool, together with the work you want reviewed, for example the text of your PRD, or a screenshot of your design. Frequently asked questions


What am I looking at?

There are 147 cards in total across 4 decks: Product Design 54, Product Management 36, Marketing 28, and UX Research 29. Each card is one criterion from a rubric, with what to look for, what good looks like, and what bad looks like.


How can this be used, and why is it useful?

    

  • Review work before it ships. Pick a card, use it to review the work in front of you, and note what good looks like and what bad looks like before you decide. Use it before a design ships, before a survey fields, before a study plan recruits, before a roadmap or launch plan commits, and before a battlecard reaches a rep. Problems are caught while they are still easy to fix, and the review is based on a check the team can point to, not on taste alone.
    • 

  • Run a team session. Deal a card, or pick one together, and use it to review a real screen, plan, or readout in front of you, in a crit, a handoff, or a first session with a new team. It works as an ice breaker because the card, not a person, is doing the judging. A junior and a senior, or design, product, and research, are all answering the same check, with the same good and bad, so everyone can speak, and a new member learns what good looks like here without being put on the spot.
    • 

  • Build a shared standard over time. Use the same cards again and again, and the team gets more consistent, the work gets more consistent, and the cards become a record of what the team has learned to check for. That is growth for the team and the product, not just one review.
    • 



How does this relate to the ProductRubric library?

The cards are derived from the library. The library is 21 rubrics, as files and through MCP, run in full. The cards take those same rubrics and break each one into its criteria, one card per criterion, plus a cover card per rubric, so you can hold, flip, print, and share one check at a time. That is why 21 rubrics become 147 cards: a rubric is one document, a card is one criterion from that document, so one rubric becomes many cards, and the card total is naturally larger than the rubric total.


Are ProductRubric cards free?

Yes. You can view all 147 cards on this page for free, with no account. You can also download a card or a whole deck as a PDF for free, from the Download button on the card or on the deck box, and print it yourself.


Can I print ProductRubric cards?

Yes. Open a card and use Download to save a PDF with the card front on the left and the card back on the right, or open a deck and use Download on the deck box to save a PDF with every card in that deck, one card per page, front and back. Print the PDF and cut the cards out.


Which decks are included?

Four decks, 147 cards in total. Product Design has 54 cards, Product Management has 36, Marketing has 28, and UX Research has 29. Each deck covers the rubrics in that area, for example Product Design covers Accessibility, Attention, Copy, Design Principles, Design Quality, Five Second, Inclusive Design, and Usability.


How do I use a ProductRubric card with an AI tool?

Open a card, copy the prompt from the Use this card with an AI tool section, paste it into an AI tool with the work attached or pasted, and read the verdict against what good looks like and what bad looks like on the card back. For the full rubrics, not just one card, connect your AI tool to the ProductRubric MCP server instead: that lets the tool read and run a whole rubric itself, with no account, no key, and nothing to install. See how to connect the MCP server on the homepage, where you can copy the server address and follow the steps for Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, Cursor, Muse, and other tools.
